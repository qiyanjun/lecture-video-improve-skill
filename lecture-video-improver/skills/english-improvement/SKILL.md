# English Improvement for Video Content

Guidelines for improving English in video scripts, transcripts, and subtitles.

## Core Principles

- **Preserve the speaker's voice**: Improve language without erasing personality or intent
- **Spoken first**: Video content is heard, not read — prioritize natural, speakable English over formal prose
- **Minimal intervention**: Make the fewest changes needed to achieve clear, correct, natural English
- **Context-aware tone**: A casual tutorial needs different language than a corporate training video

## Five Improvement Types

### 1. Grammar & Punctuation
Fix: subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, article usage (a/an/the), preposition errors,
run-on sentences, comma splices, missing or misplaced punctuation.
Preserve: deliberate stylistic fragments used for rhythm or emphasis.

### 2. Natural Fluency
Rewrite awkward or non-native phrasing so it flows naturally in English. Common patterns to fix:
- Unnatural word order ("I will you show how to…" → "I'll show you how to…")
- Overly literal phrasing from another language
- Repetitive sentence structure throughout a paragraph
- Wordy phrases ("due to the fact that" → "because", "in order to" → "to")

### 3. Vocabulary & Word Choice
- Replace vague words with more precise ones when context is clear
- Fix unnatural collocations (word combinations that don't sound right in English)
- Do NOT swap simple words for complex synonyms — clarity beats impressiveness

### 4. Clarity & Conciseness
- Break up long, tangled sentences into shorter ones
- Remove filler phrases that add no meaning
- Ensure pronoun references are unambiguous

### 5. Tone & Register
Match the tone to the content:
- Tutorial / educational: clear, direct, encouraging
- Professional / corporate: formal but not stiff
- Casual / lifestyle / YouTube: conversational, warm, energetic

## Output Format

Always produce:
1. The fully corrected content
2. A changelog grouped by improvement type, with brief before → after examples for significant edits
